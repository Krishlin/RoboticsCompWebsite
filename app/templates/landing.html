Two robots. One hill. Three minutes.

The STEMsters Robotics Competition. An autonomous robotics competition for middle and high school teams in Southern California.

Register a team Read the game manual

Summit

Summit is the SRC game for 2026. Two autonomous robots, one red and one blue, compete on a small raised hill. A robot wins by pushing its opponent off the Hill, or by being higher on the Hill when time expires. Once a match begins, no one may touch or control them.

10 cm and 500 g
Robots fit within 10 cm by 10 cm at the start and weigh 500 g or less. No height limit, and after the start signal they may expand without restriction.
A micro:bit, and your code
A micro:bit running the team's own code controls the robot. Every other part is the team's choice.
Three minutes, by radio
Matches last three minutes. The referee's field controller starts and stops both robots at once.
Quals, then a bracket
Every team plays the same number of qualification matches, then the field seeds into a single-elimination bracket.

The Hill

A 3D-printed PLA mound, 42 cm across, rising 2 cm to a flat top. Each ring is a different colour so that a colour sensor can identify where the robot is.

The kit is a Wukong expansion board with a built-in battery, two continuous-rotation motors, an ultrasonic sensor for finding the opponent, a colour sensor for reading the zone, LEGO Technic chassis parts, and the starter code. It is programmed in MakeCode. You supply a micro:bit, a laptop, and a small screwdriver.

What to bring

  • Your robot, with the official start/stop code loaded and your team number entered
  • A laptop with MakeCode and a USB cable
  • Your charger
  • A completed consent and release form for each student
  • Your team's adult, for the whole day

Awards

Every award winner receives a trophy, and the Champion takes home the Summit banner. There are no cash prizes.

Champion
The bracket winner, middle school or high school.
Middle School Champion
The middle school team that went furthest in the bracket, ties broken by qualification rank. If a middle school team wins the Championship, this goes to the next-best middle school team.
Excellence
One at each level, for the team that best explains how it designed, tested, and improved its robot, and how it helped other teams. Judged by a short interview at your table. Nothing to write or submit.
Sportsmanship
For the team that best lives the Climb together rule all day.

The exhibition field

A second Hill runs king-of-the-hill all afternoon: the winner stays on and takes the next challenger. Open to any robot that has passed inspection, including teams knocked out of the bracket. Results don't count for anything. Bring a grudge.

Who goes where

Team area: rostered students and your adult only. Spectator area: everyone else, and everyone is welcome. Field: the two handlers and the referees.
